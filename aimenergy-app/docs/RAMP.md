# RAMP shape templates

`dist/data/ramp-profiles.json` is generated by executing the open-source
[RAMP](https://github.com/RAMP-project/RAMP) Python package (`rampdemand`), using
the explicit appliance definitions in `scripts/generate_ramp_profiles.py`.
The browser reads this cached JSON offline; it does not need Python, a cloud
account, an API key, or a Python model file.

These are **illustrative active-day timing shapes, not calibrated demand
forecasts**. RAMP supplies a stochastic timing pattern. The AimEnergy scenario
engine supplies monthly kWh separately (including its household scenario input).
An appliance rating below does not override that monthly energy scenario.

## Generation and interpretation

Each category contains one representative user. RAMP simulates 30 days at
one-minute resolution, starting 1 January 2026. All appliances are enabled on
every simulated day; school holidays, weekly closure, seasons, outages and
unserved demand are not modeled. Business and institutional profiles therefore
represent an active day; the energy scenario must account for operating days.

The generator seeds both Python `random` and NumPy, using base seed 20261030
plus the category's insertion-order index. It sums 60 one-minute power samples
per hourly energy bin, averages those bins across the 30 days, and divides by
total daily energy. Every `hourlyShares` array has 24 finite, nonnegative values
and sums to 1. Index 0 is local clock hour 00:00–01:00. There is no timezone or
daylight-saving adjustment. Hourly shares describe energy allocation; they do
not represent appliance rated power, instantaneous peak, or a diversity study.

The JSON records package/dependency versions, seed, number of runs, generator
SHA-256, and every appliance assumption. A mean profile smooths random events;
it cannot describe an individual day's extremes. Reusing the cached mean across
many connections does not independently randomize their switch-on events.

## Assumed appliances

Power is watts per appliance; on-time is the target total minutes per day.
Windows are available local clock intervals, not a guarantee of continuous use.
RAMP uses 15% duration variability and 5% window variability throughout. Minimum
on-times are specified per appliance in the script/JSON. Other RAMP behavior
uses the package defaults (including stochastic coincidence for multiple units).

| Category | Assumed appliance quantities × power; target daily on-time; available windows |
| --- | --- |
| Household | 4 × 7 W LEDs, 180 min, 05:00–07:00 & 18:00–23:00; 1 × 40 W TV, 120 min, 18:00–23:00; 2 × 5 W phone chargers, 120 min, 06:00–08:00 & 17:00–23:00; 1 × 10 W radio, 90 min, 06:00–09:00 & 17:00–22:00 |
| Mill | 1 × 7,500 W motor, 180 min, 08:00–12:00 & 13:00–17:00; 4 × 20 W task lights, 120 min, 07:00–10:00 & 16:00–19:00 |
| Refrigeration | 2 × 150 W compressors, 600 min, 00:00–24:00; 2 × 9 W shop lights, 240 min, 16:00–22:00 |
| Salon | 1 × 15 W clippers, 180 min; 1 × 45 W fan, 300 min (both 08:00–18:00); 3 × 10 W LEDs, 360 min, 08:00–19:00; 1 × 1,000 W dryer, 60 min, 09:00–18:00 |
| Welding | 1 × 3,000 W welder at effective arc-on power, 120 min; 1 × 900 W grinder, 90 min (both 08:00–12:00 & 13:00–17:00); 2 × 20 W task lights, 360 min, 08:00–18:00 |
| School | 12 × 10 W LEDs, 240 min, 07:00–17:00; 4 × 35 W laptops, 180 min; 1 × 50 W printer, 30 min (both 08:00–16:00) |

Refrigeration uses equivalent stochastic compressor on-time, not a thermal or
thermostat model. Welder power excludes a detailed electrical duty cycle, motor
starting surge, power factor, and phase balancing. These assumed archetypes
require a local appliance inventory, operating schedules, seasonal evidence and
metered validation before investment or engineering use.

## Reproduce

From this app directory in PowerShell, use an isolated environment:

```powershell
py -3.12 -m venv .venv
.venv/Scripts/python.exe -m pip install --use-feature=truststore --no-compile rampdemand==0.5.0 numpy==1.26.4 pandas==2.2.2
.venv/Scripts/python.exe scripts/generate_ramp_profiles.py
```

The checked-in provenance lists the Python and numerical dependency versions
used for this artifact. Match them to reproduce bytes; a newer dependency or
RAMP release may change output. RAMP 0.5.0 is incompatible with NumPy 2.x
(its window API converts a one-element array to a scalar); keep the NumPy pin.
The generator never disables TLS certificate
verification. For an environment using a corporate CA, configure its trusted
certificate chain rather than bypassing verification.

Use `--runs` (minimum 30), `--seed` and `--output` to create another explicit
scenario. Keep the seed and input definitions with the output. The JSON's
generator hash changes when the script changes. The file intentionally omits a
wall-clock generation timestamp so identical inputs produce identical bytes.

## Upstream reference

The implementation follows the official [simple-appliance example](https://rampdemand.readthedocs.io/en/latest/examples/simple_bulb/simple_bulb.html),
[appliance parameter API](https://rampdemand.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api_document/ramp.Appliance.__init__.html)
and [daily generation API](https://rampdemand.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api_document/ramp.UseCase.generate_daily_load_profiles.html).
RAMP is distributed under [EUPL 1.2 or later](https://github.com/RAMP-project/RAMP).
For research use, the upstream documentation requests citing F. Lombardi et al.,
*Generating high-resolution multi-energy load profiles for remote areas with an
open-source stochastic model*, Energy (2019),
[DOI: 10.1016/j.energy.2019.04.097](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.energy.2019.04.097).
